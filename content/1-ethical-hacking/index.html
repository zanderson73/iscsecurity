Ethical Hacking

A comprehensive operational curriculum that rebuilds the complete adversary lifecycle, teaching you to predict, emulate, and neutralize sophisticated threat actors.

Operational Philosophy

This curriculum is built on a single premise: You cannot reliably defend against an adversary you do not understand. Every module reconstructs the decision cycle of a sophisticated threat actor, how they observe, prioritize, adapt, and persist. We do not teach tools in isolation; we teach tradecraft. You will learn to think with the attacker's anticipation, move with their patience, and evade with their creativity, all within lawful, scoped, and authorized boundaries.
The outcome is not just technical competence, but adversarial intuition: the ability to predict exploitation paths before they are weaponized.

Module Overview

This module mirrors the full operational lifecycle of an advanced threat actor across 21 progressive phases. You will begin with passive reconnaissance and OSINT tradecraft, learning how adversaries build target intelligence without sending a single packet. From there, you will progress through systematic footprinting, enumeration, and vulnerability analysis, internalizing how attackers prioritize low-noise, high-value entry points.

Mid-curriculum, you step into the active kill chain: system exploitation, malware delivery, social engineering, session hijacking, and network sniffing, each examined from the attacker's cost-benefit perspective. You will then study evasion (IDS, firewalls, honeypots), followed by specialized attack surfaces: web applications, SQL injection, wireless and mobile platforms, IoT/OT, and cloud environments. The program closes with cryptography, reinforcing how adversaries protect their own communications and data and how you counter those protections.

Every lesson pairs adversarial mindset coaching with verified proof-of-concept commands. The objective is operability.

By the final module, you should be able to plan, execute, and report a full-scope authorized engagement with the discipline and foresight of a seasoned red team operator.

Learning Objectives

  • Internalize the adversary decision cycle to predict attacker behavior before it manifests in telemetry.
  • Master open source intelligence (OSINT) gathering with the operational security (OPSEC) discipline of a reconnaissance-focused threat actor.
  • Conduct footprinting, reconnaissance, and enumeration against authorized targets using low-noise tradecraft.
  • Perform vulnerability analysis to identify weaknesses from the attacker's cost-benefit and prioritization perspective.
  • Execute system hacking methodologies and privilege escalation while maintaining persistence and situational awareness.
  • Analyze malware, sniffing attacks, and social engineering through the lens of adversary intent and targeting logic.
