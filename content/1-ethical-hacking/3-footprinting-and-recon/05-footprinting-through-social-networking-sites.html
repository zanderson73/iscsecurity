1. Overview & Threat Landscape

Social networking services (SNS) are high-yield reconnaissance targets because they expose the human attack surface ; names, roles, relationships, sentiment, geolocation, and organisational structure. Modern adversaries (APTs, ransomware affiliates, and BEC groups) do not merely "browse profiles." They automate large-scale correlation, weaponise AI-generated deepfakes for pretexting, and pivot from personal leaks into corporate credentials.

Common platforms of intelligence value include LinkedIn, X (Twitter), Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, Reddit, Strava, and GitHub. Many posts are indexed by search engines or archived by third parties (Wayback Machine, Shadow Library), allowing anonymous, non-authenticated collection even if the original post is later deleted.

RedOps/BlueOps: Every piece of data you can harvest as a Red Operator is a vulnerability a Blue Operator must learn to strip, obscure, or monitor. Reconnaissance is a precursor to phishing, vishing, physical intrusion, and supply-chain compromise.

2. Adversarial Methodology: Phased Recon

Real-world operators follow a phased approach rather than ad-hoc searching.

  1. Phase 1: Target Scoping & Infrastructure Mapping; Identify target domains, naming conventions, and official corporate social presence.
  2. Phase 2: Passive Enumeration (No Authentication); Search engine dorking, archived content, public APIs, and aggregator sites. No direct platform interaction occurs.
  3. Phase 3: Active Enumeration (Authenticated / Sock Puppet); Create and age alias accounts to view connection graphs, comment histories, and "friends-only" content.
  4. Phase 4: Content Extraction & Pivoting; Download media for EXIF analysis, correlate usernames across platforms, and extract geolocation markers.
  5. Phase 5: Relationship Graphing; Build organisational charts from connection metadata (who follows whom, endorsements, tags).
  6. Phase 6: Intelligence Fusion & Weaponisation Prep; Correlate findings with breach data, job boards, and DNS records to build pretexts for spear-phishing or vishing.

6. Social Media Analytics, Trends, and Content Discovery

Beyond profile enumeration, operators analyse content to determine organisational culture, technology stack mentions, travel itineraries, and grievances that can be weaponised. Modern workflows combine Google Dorks, snscrape (for X/Twitter without API keys), and BuzzSumo-style tools.

6.1 Technique: Google Dorking for Social Content

# Find PDFs shared on LinkedIn by target domain employees
site:linkedin.com/in "MegaCorp" filetype:pdf

# Find exposed phone numbers or emails in Instagram bios
site:instagram.com "MegaCorp" "email" OR "call me" OR "contact"

# Find employees discussing internal tools on X/Twitter
site:twitter.com OR site:x.com "MegaCorp" ("Jira" OR "Confluence" OR "VPN down")

6.2 Tool: snscrape (Non-API Twitter/X Scraping)

$ snscrape --jsonl --max-results 200 twitter-search "from:jdoe since:2023-01-01 until:2024-01-01" > jdoe_tweets.jsonl
$ cat jdoe_tweets.jsonl | jq -r '.content'

"Just finished a sprint deploying the new Palo Alto firewalls at MegaCorp HQ #cybersecurity"
"Loving the new Okta SSO rollout... mostly."
"Flying out to London next week for the datacenter migration. ✈️ #MegaCorp"

Safety / Accuracy Warning:

  • Social-media hygiene training for high-value employees

  • Out-of-band verification for any travel-related or infrastructure-change requests

8. Case Studies

Case Study 1: APT29 (Cozy Bear): LinkedIn Spear-Phishing

In operations targeting global think tanks and government contractors, APT29 created fake LinkedIn profiles posing as recruiters for well-known defence firms. After establishing connections with target employees, they sent malicious "job description" PDFs and links to credential-harvesting domains themed to the victim's current role. The social reconnaissance phase (profile cloning, connection mapping, and role tailoring) took weeks, but the resulting compromise provided persistent access to sensitive policy documents.

Connection requests from "recruiters" with mutual connections bypass mental trust boundaries. Blue teams must monitor for executive impersonation profiles and train staff to verify recruiter identities out-of-band.

Case Study 2: Strava & Instagram Geolocation: Military Base Exposure

In 2018, a publicly available "Strava Heatmap" revealed the running routes of military personnel on classified bases because soldiers shared fitness data to social platforms. Cross-referencing Strava usernames with Instagram handles exposed real names, faces, and base locations. No "hack" was required, only correlation of intentionally public data.

Operational Security (OPSEC) for personnel must include geolocation-tagging policies and regular social-media hygiene audits, not just network firewalls.

Case Study 3: Data Broker Aggregation; The Clearview AI Model

Clearview AI scraped billions of public-facing social media images without consent to build a facial-recognition database sold to law enforcement and private entities. While not a traditional "breach," it demonstrated that public social data can be weaponised at scale via automated scraping and ML classification, raising legal questions under GDPR Article 9 (biometric data).

Organisations must treat public profile pictures and posts as sensitive data subject to retention and privacy regulations; defensive teams should request removal from aggregator indexes where possible.

9. Analysis, Correlation, and Dossier Building

Raw tool output is noise until correlated. Use the following triage workflow:

  1. Username Normalisation: Strip platform-specific suffixes to generate a master list of handles (e.g., @jdoe90 on Twitter likely equals jdoe on GitHub).
  2. Timeline Reconstruction: Sort posts by date to identify travel windows, project milestones, or equipment upgrades.
  3. Geolocation Clustering: Map EXIF coordinates and geotags to infer office locations, commute routes, and frequented venues (coffee shops near the target are ideal vishing pretexts).
  4. Relationship Mapping: Import follower/following lists into Maltego or Obsidian to visualise organisational charts and identify high-centrality targets (executive assistants often have more access than VPs).
  5. Credential Pivot: Feed discovered usernames/emails into breach databases (e.g., Have I Been Pwned API, DeHashed) to identify password reuse or weak MFA enrolment.

10. OPSEC & Legal Boundaries

10.1 Operational Security for Red Teams

  • Sock Puppets: Create accounts at least 30–60 days before an engagement. Age them with organic content, followers, and photo uploads to pass platform bot-detection.
  • Attribution Segregation: Run recon from dedicated VMs or cloud workstations (non-corporate IP ranges). Use residential proxies for platforms with aggressive anti-bot measures.
  • Compartmentalisation: Never reuse a sock puppet across multiple clients. Burn accounts and associated phone numbers after the engagement.
  • Anti-Forensics: Scrape during business hours of the target's timezone to blend with normal traffic. Avoid aggressive thread counts that trigger rate-limiting.

10.2 Legal & Compliance Boundaries

Jurisdiction / Framework Relevant Constraint Risk to Operator
CFAA (USA) "Exceeding authorised access" on a protected computer (including ToS violations in some circuits). Criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment.
GDPR (EU) Article 9 (special category data); automated scraping of bios, images, and preferences may constitute unlawful processing. Administrative fines up to 4% global turnover; civil liability.
Computer Misuse Act 1990 (UK) Section 1: Unauthorised access. Scraping behind a login wall without consent may qualify. Criminal conviction, up to 2 years imprisonment.
Platform ToS Most SNS explicitly prohibit automated scraping in their terms. Civil litigation, permanent IP/account bans, CFAA exposure.
Golden Rule (ROE):
If you are not certain the activity is within signed Rules of Engagement (RoE) and legal review, do not execute it. When in doubt, use only passive, unauthenticated techniques.

11. Quick Triaging Checklist: Social Recon Workflow

□ Scope Confirmation
    □ Client has authorised SNS reconnaissance in writing.
    □ Platform list and depth limits defined (passive vs. authenticated).
    □ "No-touch" personnel list (e.g., C-suite only by name, no contact).

□ Infrastructure Setup
    □ Dedicated recon VM or cloud workspace provisioned.
    □ Residential proxy / VPN configured and tested.
    □ Sock-puppet accounts aged and compartmentalised.

□ Phase 1 — Passive Discovery
    □ Google/Bing dorks executed for public profile exposure.
    □ theHarvester run for emails and hosts.
    □ Wayback / archive.org checked for deleted corporate posts.
    □ Username candidates generated via linkedin2username.

□ Phase 2 — Active Enumeration
    □ Sherlock / Maigret executed for cross-platform correlation.
    □ holehe run to validate email-to-platform mappings.
    □ snscrape / API queries for post content and timeline.
    □ Instagram / TikTok checked for geotags and follower leaks.

□ Phase 3 — Enrichment
    □ Images downloaded and EXIF analysed (see image_exif tool).
    □ Follower graphs imported into Maltego / Obsidian.
    □ Posts keyword-searched for technology stacks, travel, grievances.
    □ Breach correlation completed (HIBP, DeHashed).

□ Phase 4 — Sanitisation & Reporting
    □ Raw data encrypted at rest (AES-256).
    □ Personal data minimised (report only what is risk-relevant).
    □ Sock-puppet accounts deactivated or burned.
    □ Engagement logs retained per client data-retention policy.

□ Blue-Team Handoff
    □ Defensive recommendations appended (see Section 13).
    □ Indicators of Exposure (IoE) list provided for remediation.

13. Defensive Countermeasures & Threat Mapping

13.1 Blue Team Mitigations

  • Executive & Employee Digital Hygiene Training: Teach staff to review privacy settings quarterly, remove EXIF data before posting, and avoid tagging corporate buildings.
  • Honeypot / Canary Profiles: Create fake executive or HR profiles seeded with unique contact details. Monitor for connection requests and scraping attempts as an early-warning system.
  • Brand Protection Monitoring: Use services (e.g., PhishLabs, ZeroFox) to detect impersonation accounts and typosquat domains registered within hours of social posts mentioning new projects.
  • Metadata Sanitisation at the Gateway: Deploy email and web proxies that strip EXIF geolocation from all images uploaded to corporate assets or email.
  • Relationship Graph Poisoning: Limit connection visibility on LinkedIn to "1st degree only." Restrict who can see your follower/following lists on X and Instagram.
  • Rate-Limit & Bot Detection: If operating a corporate social presence, configure WAF/CDN rules to block aggressive scrapers and abnormal User-Agent strings.

13.2 Threat Mapping: Recon → Attack Chain

Recon Output Likely Follow-on Attack Defensive Control
Employee email + role list Spear-phishing / BEC External email banners; MFA (FIDO2); pretext simulation.
Technology stack mentions (e.g., "new Okta portal") Credential stuffing / phishing themed to SSO Brand monitoring for fake SSO portals; IdP anomaly alerts.
Geolocation tags / travel itineraries Physical tailing / USB drop / vishing to hotel front desk Travel OPSEC briefings; "no location tagging" policy.
Org chart from connection graph Whaling / supply-chain impersonation Out-of-band verification for wire transfers and sensitive requests.
Correlated breach password + work email Password spray / account takeover Dark-web monitoring; forced password resets on breach correlation.
Blue Team Note:
Indirect intelligence sources are where organisations have the least visibility but the most exposure. You cannot firewall social media, but you can control what those platforms reveal. Data hygiene is perimeter defence for the OSINT age.

14. Ethical Considerations and Engagement Scope

Social network reconnaissance straddles the line between open-source intelligence gathering and invasive surveillance. Authorised ethical operators must adhere to the following immutable principles:

  • Explicit Written Authorisation: The client must define the target individuals, platforms, and depth of reconnaissance. "Grey area" does not exist if it is not in the RoE, it is out of scope.
  • Data Minimisation: Collect only what is necessary to demonstrate risk. Delete personal data irrelevant to the security narrative.
  • No Interference: Do not interact with targets (no DMs, comments, likes, or connection requests) unless explicitly required and approved under a social-engineering workstream.
  • No Credential Abuse: Discovering a reused password from a breach does not authorise you to test it against live systems without explicit permission.
  • Reporting for Defense: The end goal is risk reduction. Every recon finding must be paired with a measurable defensive countermeasure.
